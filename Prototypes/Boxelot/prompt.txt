                Another simpler approach: maintain a set of tiles that are currently “clickable” (initially the ones adjacent to the starting tile). When a monster is revealed, do not add its neighbors to the clickable set until it’s dead.

                    If it’s an empty or loot tile, then after revealing it, mark it as cleared and add its neighbors to the list of revealable tiles (unless those neighbors are themselves monsters – actually, you can allow revealing a monster tile as well, just revealing doesn’t kill it).

                    If it’s a monster, reveal it (now visible), but do not automatically reveal its neighbors yet; wait until it’s killed to then consider its neighbors.

            If the tile is not adjacent to a revealed clear tile (i.e. not reachable yet), ignore the click (or provide a sound feedback indicating it’s not allowed).

    When revealing a tile, update its state and appearance:

        Flip the tile’s color or sprite to indicate it’s now visible.

        If empty: maybe change the tile color to a lighter shade or put a small dot or floor texture to show it’s cleared ground.

        If enemy:

            Show a colored square (e.g. red) or an icon to represent the monster. Include a text label of its HP and attack (for example “5/5 HP, 2 ATK” or small heart and sword symbols).

            You can update this text dynamically during combat as HP changes.

            The monster remains on the tile until defeated, acting as an obstacle.

        If loot (treasure or item):

            Reveal an icon or text for the item. For a chest, you might display a chest icon and optionally immediately “open” it to give random loot.

            If it’s a direct item (like a potion lying on the ground), you could automatically give it to the player on reveal or require the player to click it to pick it up. (Original Dungelot auto-collected most items on reveal.)

            Simpler: auto-collect on reveal. E.g., “You found a health potion!” – add to inventory, remove the tile (tile becomes an empty floor after collection).

            Or for a chest: when revealed, you could either immediately roll its content and give it, or require a second click to open. Given the simplicity goal, immediate reward is fine.

            After collecting, that tile becomes cleared (no longer contains an item).

        If trap:

            Trigger its effect on the player immediately upon reveal. E.g., reduce player HP by X, show a message “It’s a trap! You lost X HP.”

            Possibly mark the tile as cleared (the trap is expended).

            If a trap could have ongoing effect (like poison status), implement a status on the player that causes damage over a few turns, but this might be beyond core scope. A simple instant damage trap is enough.

        If door:

            The door at start is locked. If the player clicks it without the key, nothing happens (maybe a “locked” message).

            If the player has obtained the key, clicking the door will transition to the next floor (generate new floor).

            You can also visually indicate the door unlocking when the key is acquired (e.g. change its color or icon).

    After revealing a tile and handling its outcome (loot collection, trap damage, etc.), if that reveal leads to new accessible tiles, make them clickable:

        If an empty or loot tile was revealed and cleared, all its neighbors that are still hidden become eligible to reveal (unless blocked by another adjacent monster).

        If it was a monster, since it’s not dead yet, do not expand the frontier from that tile.

        Once a monster is defeated, then treat that tile as cleared; at that moment, you should reveal (or mark as now allowed to reveal) any still-hidden neighbors of that tile (since the obstacle is removed).

        This can be implemented by queueing reveals or simply by checking adjacency each click as earlier described.

5. Combat Mechanics

Implement the combat turn logic such that clicking an enemy performs an attack round:

    Distinguish between clicking a hidden tile vs a revealed enemy tile. You might use different input handlers or a condition in one handler based on tile state:

        If the tile clicked is a revealed enemy and that enemy is alive (HP > 0), then treat the click as the player's decision to attack that enemy.

    Attack Round Sequence:

        Enemy strikes first: Subtract the enemy’s attack value from the player’s HP​
        .

            If you have enemy special abilities, handle those here. For example, if enemy has “doubleAttackChance” and the chance triggers, subtract twice its attack (or do two separate hits). If enemy has poison attack, apply a status, etc.

            Update the UI for player HP. Possibly flash the player’s HP or play a damage sound for feedback.

            If player HP <= 0 after this hit, the player dies – trigger Game Over (see step 7 for Game Over handling) and end combat immediately (the enemy might survive since player died).

        Player strikes back: If the player is still alive, subtract the player’s attack value from the enemy’s HP.

            If the player has any special effects (not much mentioned in original beyond maybe class perks), apply those if any (for example, if the hero had a lifesteal ability, you’d heal the player for some amount of damage dealt – only if you implemented such a perk).

            Update the enemy’s displayed HP on its tile.

            If enemy HP <= 0, the enemy is defeated:

                Remove or hide the enemy’s sprite on that tile. You might replace it with a corpse icon or simply mark the tile as cleared floor now.

                Give the player any rewards from that enemy:

                    If this enemy had the key, mark the player as having obtained the key (and possibly show a key icon in the UI).

                    The enemy might drop loot like a small amount of gold or even an item (e.g. a small chance to drop a potion or spell). If you want, implement drops: for instance, if enemy dies, roll random chance for a drop, and if so, add that item to the inventory or auto-use it. (This is an optional enhancement; the game already has plenty of loot on tiles, but a little gold drop or occasional item can make combat more rewarding.)

                Award the player XP for the kill. E.g., each enemy could give a certain XP (maybe equal to its base attack or a fixed amount based on difficulty). Increase player XP and check for level-up:

                    If player’s XP >= threshold for next level, increment player level and improve stats (e.g. +10 max HP and fully heal, +1 attack, etc.). Display a “Level Up!” message and update stat UI.

                Since the enemy is gone, the tile is now clear. This means previously blocked adjacent tiles are now free to be revealed. So, reveal any neighbors of this tile that should become accessible. (You can either automatically flip any that are safe to flip or simply allow the player to click them now. Original game typically required manual flipping; it didn’t auto-reveal empty tiles in a flood fill – except the initial click may have revealed an area until a monster stops it. If you want, you can implement a Minesweeper-like flood reveal for contiguous empty tiles for convenience, but manual reveal is okay.)

                If defeating this enemy means the player now has the key and the door was already revealed (visible), the player can now click the door to proceed. If the door wasn’t revealed yet (because maybe you placed it hidden somewhere), the player would have to still find it by revealing tiles.

        If the enemy is still alive and the player is still alive, combat is not over. The player can choose to attack again (another click). You could also allow the player to use an item or decide to reveal other tiles (if available) before finishing off the enemy, but typically once engaged it’s kill or be killed. However, the game doesn’t force lock-in combat; if there are other reachable tiles not blocked by this enemy, the player could theoretically go flip those first (especially if the enemy isn’t physically blocking the path to them).

            In Dungelot, since monsters physically block the path, you usually have to kill them to move on. But if two monsters are side by side, one might block certain tiles and the other blocks others; you could attack one or the other in any order.

            We will not implement a concept of moving the hero or running away from a fight; however, leaving a monster alive is possible if you don’t need to clear beyond it (e.g., you got the key and can reach the door without killing every monster)​
            .

    Multiple Enemies: The player can only attack one enemy at a time (the one whose tile they click). Other revealed enemies do not automatically attack the player unless engaged. The original game’s monsters don’t move; they only deal damage when you attack them (except perhaps some special cases). So you do not need a global timer for enemies attacking – only on the player’s initiation do they trade blows.

    Provide feedback during combat:

        Update HP numbers on UI immediately.

        Show perhaps a quick animation or flash on the enemy or player sprite when they take damage.

        You can also show floating damage text (e.g. “-2” above the character) for polish.

        If implementing special moves, make sure to message them (like “Goblin strikes twice!”).

    Combat ends when either the enemy is dead or the player is dead. If the player wins, they continue exploring (or move to next floor if they choose). If the player dies, game over (handled in section 7).

6. Inventory and Item Use

Implement the inventory system so the player can effectively use items they collect:

    Inventory Representation: You could have a fixed slot UI (say 5 or 6 slots visible on screen) and when an item is picked up, put an icon in the first empty slot. Alternatively, simply list them or show small images with a quantity if stackable. Simplicity: use a horizontal or vertical list of small rectangle icons at a side of the screen.

    Item Objects: Define how each item works when used:

        Example item implementations:

            Health Potion: Using it restores a certain amount of HP to the player (not above max HP). If the player is at max or near max, you might allow overheal to increase max HP slightly or just cap at max.

            Attack Boost (Sword): Could be automatically applied on pickup (immediately increase attack stat and perhaps not even appear in inventory because it’s not a usable item, more a permanent stat increase). Or if kept in inventory, using it could consume the item and grant +1 attack permanently. The original game auto-applied swords and hearts when found​
            , so you can simply do auto stat increase on reveal for these types.

            Spell Scroll: Using it should prompt for target if needed. You can implement usage in a few ways:

                Easiest: when player clicks the scroll in inventory, check if an enemy is currently revealed. If exactly one enemy is present (or one particularly is targeted/selected), apply the spell effect to that enemy directly. If multiple enemies are available, you might need the player to pick which one. A possible approach: after clicking the spell, set a state like pendingSpell = {type: 'Fireball', damage:10} and highlight enemies, allowing the next enemy click to apply the spell instead of a normal attack. If no enemy is clicked and the player changes mind, they could click the spell again to cancel perhaps.

                If implementing a stun or area effect spell, handle accordingly (e.g. freeze could set an enemy’s stunned status which you check in combat to skip its attack for one turn).

            Spell Sacrifice for Health: Provide a way to use the spell on oneself to heal instead of casting on an enemy. This could be a separate button or option. For instance, right-click the spell (on desktop) to consume it for healing, or have a small “Heal” button next to it. Alternatively, when clicking the spell, you could pop up a small confirmation UI: “Use Fireball: [Cast on enemy] or [Sacrifice for +5 HP]”. The player then chooses.

                Implement this choice simply: if the player chooses to sacrifice, increase player HP (not exceeding max, or maybe exceeding temporarily as an overheal if you want to allow it). Remove the spell from inventory.

                If cast on enemy, apply damage to enemy: subtract from enemy HP (possibly kill it outright if damage >= HP). We should still simulate the combat turn properly (for fairness, you might have the spell effect happen on the player’s turn without triggering an immediate enemy counter-attack, since using an item is usually instead of normal attack). In Dungelot, casting a spell is an action that typically would eliminate or weaken an enemy without the enemy hitting first (this can be a tactical advantage).

    Inventory UI Interaction:

        Set all inventory item icons to be interactive (setInteractive()) so they can be clicked/tapped.

        On hover (desktop), show a tooltip with item name/effect. You can implement this by having a text object that you position near the cursor or in a fixed tooltip area. On pointerover of item icon, set the text to the item’s description; on pointerout, clear the text.

        On click, implement logic:

            If item is a direct use (potion, scroll), perform its action as described above.

            Update player stats or enemy stats based on the item effect.

            Remove the item from inventory if consumed. You might then shift the remaining items in the UI or leave an empty slot.

            If item cannot be used (for example, trying to cast a spell when no enemies are present), you might show a message “No target for this spell right now” and not consume it.

        Ensure using items can be done at any appropriate time:

            The player should be able to use a potion in between fights or even during a fight (which would presumably take a turn – in original Dungelot, using an item like a spell is effectively your action instead of attacking, and enemies don’t hit you first when you drink a potion or cast a spell, IIRC, but you can decide the turn handling).

            Simplicity: allow item use freely when not actively in the middle of an attack click. For example, if an enemy is revealed but you haven’t clicked to attack this turn yet, you could drink a potion to heal before clicking the enemy to attack. You may need to consider if an enemy is revealed and blocking progress, can you reveal other tiles or use items? Yes, you should be able to use items anytime out of combat rounds.

            If you want more strict turn structure, you could consider using an item as the player’s turn (enemy might get to attack if you’re “in combat range”). But since movement is not spatial here, it’s okay to allow item use at will.

    Keep the inventory UI updated: after using or picking up items, refresh the display (remove used items, add new ones).

7. Progression to Next Floor

    When the player has the key (after killing the key-bearing enemy) and the exit door tile is revealed, allow them to go to the next floor:

        On clicking the door tile (which was locked before), detect if player.hasKey == true.

        If yes, possibly show a brief summary (“Floor X cleared! Moving to floor X+1…”) or just immediately transition.

        Transition could simply be:

            Increase currentFloor counter.

            Call the generateFloor(newFloor) function to create the next level.

            You may choose to keep the same scene and just wipe the old grid and create a new one. Make sure to reset the necessary state: the player keeps their stats (HP (carry over current HP), maxHP, attack, inventory, level, etc. should persist to the next floor), but you should reset things like player.hasKey to false for the new floor, and initialize the new grid.

            Alternatively, you could destroy the old tile sprites and create new ones for the new floor. Phaser’s Scene can be kept, just remove or reuse objects.

        Increase difficulty for the new floor:

            You can ramp up enemy stats a bit (some games scale enemy HP/attack by floor count or introduce new enemy types on certain floors).

            E.g., for floor 5+, allow mid-tier enemies; floor 10 = boss; beyond 10, increase all enemies’ HP by a factor, etc.

            You might maintain a list of possible enemy types per floor range, or simply make a formula to buff their stats.

        If a boss floor is reached, generate one big boss enemy (and maybe a couple smaller adds or traps). The key could be held by the boss as well, or if it’s final floor, the key is not needed.

        Continue this until either the player dies (game over) or a final victory condition if you decide one (like after boss, if you want to end the game).

        Tip: When moving to a new floor, it might be wise to fully heal the player or heal a bit (some roguelikes give a small heal on level up or floor clear). Dungelot often leaves you with your remaining HP, making each floor riskier. To keep it from being too punishing, maybe let the player carry over current HP as is, but the items and level-ups they got hopefully sustain them.

8. Game Over and Restart

    Game Over Trigger: As stated, if player HP <= 0, initiate game over:

        Stop any further input that could affect the game state. (E.g., disable tile clicks, etc., or simply check in handlers that game is not over before proceeding.)

        Display a Game Over overlay: perhaps a semi-transparent dark overlay with a large text “Game Over” and the floor reached or score.

        Show two buttons: Restart and Exit.

            Restart: This should reset the entire game progress and start a new game from floor 1.

                If you have a main menu scene, you could return to menu and then start, or directly call generateFloor(1) and reset player stats.

                Reset player stats to initial values (starting HP, attack, level 1, XP 0, empty inventory, 0 gold).

                Could also reset any global counters like score or floor number display.

                Essentially reinitialize the DungeonScene to its start state. It might be simplest to just reload the scene: this.scene.restart() in Phaser will restart the scene fresh. Alternatively, call a function that does the resets and generates floor 1.

            Exit: If a main menu is implemented, go back to the menu scene: this.scene.start('MainMenuScene'). If no menu, this could do the same as restart or simply hang on game over. But since the requirement explicitly says restart/exit, assume we have a menu or an exit functionality.

        You might want to record best score or deepest floor and show it on game over, but optional.

    Ensure that after restarting or exiting, all remnants of the previous run are cleared or reinitialized so the new game starts cleanly.
